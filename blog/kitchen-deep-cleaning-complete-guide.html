Kitchen Deep Cleaning: A Practical Grease-Cleaning Guide

Plan cabinet, hob, backsplash, sink and appliance cleaning, with preparation steps and a clear distinction between kitchen scope and chimney extras.

CleanNest Cleaning JournalUpdated 12 September 2026

Break the kitchen into separate cleaning tasks

A kitchen clean becomes easier to plan when you separate grease-covered surfaces, storage interiors, appliance exteriors and specialist appliances. Each has different access and material-care requirements. The worktop may be ready to wipe while cupboards still need emptying and a chimney needs its own assessment.

Start by listing the areas that remain dirty after routine cleaning. Photograph heavy buildup and damaged finishes. This makes it easier to agree the work without treating every kitchen as the same size or condition.

Prepare food, utensils and storage

Clear the worktop and move loose food and utensils away from the cleaning area. Empty cupboards that need internal wiping, and arrange suitable storage for perishables if refrigerator interiors are part of your plans. Decide where items will go while surfaces dry.

Identify delicate stone, timber, laminates and appliance coatings. Keep care instructions accessible and tell the team about any finish that has peeled, swollen or changed colour after previous cleaning.

Cabinet fronts, handles and backsplash grease

Grease mixed with dust can build up on handles, cabinet tops and tiles near the hob. Use cleaning products suitable for each finish and follow their directions. Begin with an appropriate gentle method rather than assuming abrasive pads or strong solvents are necessary.

Cabinet interiors need different preparation from exteriors. Ask whether shelves and drawers must be empty and whether a fixed or fragile fitting limits access. Avoid soaking joints or leaving moisture on a material whose care instructions require prompt drying.

Hob, exhaust and chimney: understand the scope

CleanNest’s kitchen service includes hob degreasing and exhaust cleaning. Chimney cleaning is an optional, separately priced service. Review the chimney scope if filters, baffles or accessible hood sections also need attention.

Flag gas-fitting or electrical problems separately. Cleaning burners and surfaces is different from repairing an appliance or working on gas connections. Confirm access and treatment with the team instead of dismantling fittings to prepare for the visit.

Sink, counters and appliance surfaces

Match sink and countertop care to the actual material. A mark may be removable buildup or permanent wear, so avoid assuming a descaler or abrasive product is suitable. Rinse or dry only as directed by the product and surface guidance.

Appliance exteriors do not automatically include the oven, refrigerator or freezer interior. If you need internal refrigerator cleaning, review the separate service. Do not pull out heavy appliances or disturb connections just to reach hidden dirt; agree access first.

Review the job before putting everything back

Check cabinet edges, backsplash corners, hob surfaces and the sink once cleaning is finished. Raise any missed area within the agreed scope with the team. Allow surfaces to dry before replacing food, utensils or stored items.

To keep the scope clear, send the kitchen size, condition photos and any optional chimney or appliance request together.
