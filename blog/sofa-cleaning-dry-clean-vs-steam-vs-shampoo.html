Sofa Cleaning: Dry Cleaning, Steam and Shampoo Explained

Compare sofa-cleaning questions that matter: fabric compatibility, method, stain limitations, drying and per-seat pricing before choosing a treatment.

The care label comes before the method name

There is no universally best sofa-cleaning method. Fabric, dyes, cushion construction, trims and the manufacturer’s care instructions all affect what is suitable. A treatment described as dry, steam or shampoo is not enough information on its own.

Photograph the care label, the whole sofa and the affected areas before requesting a quote. Mention previous cleaning, colour bleeding, leather sections and removable covers. Do not assume removable covers are machine washable or that a treatment suitable for one sofa is suitable for another.

What to ask about dry cleaning

Ask the provider what the term means for your sofa: which product is used, how it is applied and removed, and why it fits the care label. Method labels can describe different processes, so a low starting price or the word “dry” should not substitute for a material assessment.

Discuss delicate trims, adhesives and older upholstery before any product is applied. A provider should explain the proposed treatment and limitations; do not choose solely on the assumption that it will be ready for use immediately.

What to ask about steam and extraction

Ask whether the proposed method uses steam, liquid application and extraction, or another process. Request confirmation that heat and moisture are suitable for the fabric and its backing. Discuss how the cleaning residue and moisture are removed and how ventilation affects the return to use.

A steam-labelled option should not be taken as a guarantee of sterilisation, allergen removal or success on every stain. Confirm the actual scope and expected outcome for your sofa.

What to ask about shampoo or foam

If a product is worked into the upholstery, ask how it will be removed and whether any residue could remain. Check whether brushing or agitation is suitable for the pile and finish. The same questions about colourfastness and drying still apply.

A method name does not establish a quality ranking. Compare the explanation, care compatibility and work included rather than treating “premium” or “deep” as a technical specification.

Stains and drying expectations

Tell the team what caused a stain, how old it is and which products have already been used. Some marks or colour changes can remain after cleaning. Agree the treatment limits before work begins and avoid promising a result based only on a photograph.

Drying depends on the item, treatment and conditions in the room. Ask for an estimate specific to your home, keep the recommended ventilation available and wait until the sofa is dry before use. Avoid scheduling the clean immediately before guests arrive if drying time is uncertain.

Compare the CleanNest options and seat count

CleanNest lists Standard, Premium and Premium + steam options on its rate card. These are booking options; the team must confirm their suitability for your material. Send the seat count and identify chaise and recliner sections so the estimate represents the actual sofa.

Chaise sections count as two seats, recliners have a published supplement, and cushions are included with sofa cleaning. See the sofa service page for scope and preparation, then request an estimate.
